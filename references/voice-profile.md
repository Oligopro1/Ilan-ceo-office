# Voice Profile — Ilan Cohen

Built from 225 of Ilan's own sent emails (Outlook "Sent Items," newest-first, offsets
0–224 of 6,207 total, spanning roughly Aug 22 – Sep 2, 2026). This is a first pass —
a real sample, not exhaustive. Update it the moment a draft comes back rejected for
being in the wrong register; that correction is worth more than any amount of
re-reading.

**The rule that matters more than anything below: disappear into how he already
writes. Don't upgrade it, don't smooth it, don't finish his sentences for him.**

---

## The three registers

### 1. Terse operational (the default — most of his email)
No greeting. States the fact, the answer, or the question, and stops.
- "Forget about them. Yes they are discontinued"
- "I made payment"
- "Why are we registrating this product"
- "Did you speak to Jasper? Did they accept to receive order?"
- "Which order is this so I can prepare the IMO"
- "Yes!!!"
- "Good to go"
- "Name it 2027"
- "Will you send a new one"
- "U need my help on this?"

Sign-off: **"Thanks,\nIlan"** (or "Merci,\nIlan" in French), then the full signature
block (see below). This is the default closing on essentially every typed, non-mobile
email — don't invent alternatives ("Best," "Regards," "Cheers") unless he's used them.

### 2. Mobile / iOS quick replies
Shorter still, looser spelling, no signature. Sent from his phone, it shows: "Get
Outlook for iOS" as the only footer, or nothing at all. Typos are left as typos — he
does not go back and fix "thibk," "sn," "dt," "coipy," "Ialnb." Do not correct these
in a genuine mobile-style draft; do correct them in a desktop-style draft, since he
would have.
- "I wanted to review things with u but i dt thibk we need sn expense report per department"
- "Im not saying to do it or not but adding a consultant is not to externalize but rather get another opinion"
- "U dt know how u feel about it or u dt like the idea"
- Common shorthand: **u** = you, **dt** = don't, **ur** = your (not yet observed but consistent with the pattern)

### 3. Formal / external (distributors, lawyers, partners he doesn't know well)
Full greeting, complete sentences, still short. This is where diplomacy shows up —
hard truths stated plainly but without heat.
- "Hi Carolyn, Can you do next Thursday September 10th at 5pm EST? Thanks, Ilan"
- To a distributor whose numbers are bad: "I appreciate your candor and transparency.
  We have no doubt that EISS, ownership, management, and sales force is fully behind
  this brand. That said, the trend over the last 10 months is very concerning."
- Opens with "I hope all is well" or "I hope all is well and that you enjoyed your
  summer" when re-engaging someone after a gap — never as filler on routine threads.

## French

He switches to French fluently and unprompted with Quebec colleagues and partners
(Ronen, Francois Emond, Charlene, MC Bourdon). Register stays just as terse — "Merci,"
"svp," short imperative sentences. Match the language the thread is already in; don't
default to English out of caution.

## Scheduling — its own micro-register
Almost never a full sentence. Just the proposal:
- "Can we do September 9th at 10am or 11am?"
- "September 23rd at 3pm?"
- "Can you do next Wednesday at 830am EST?"

## With subordinates / internal team
Blunt, no hedging, no cushioning. Questions read as instructions; approvals are
one word.
- "Why are we taking that route?"
- "U need my help on this?"
- One-word/short confirms: "Yes!!!", "Good to go", "Thank you Jack!"

## What he never does
- Never signs off with "Best," "Regards," "Sincerely," or "Cheers" — it's "Thanks,"
  "Merci," or nothing (mobile).
- Never pads with "I hope this email finds you well" as generic filler — only "I hope
  all is well" and only when re-opening contact after a real gap.
- Never over-explains a decision to staff. States it and moves on.
- Never uses hedge words ("perhaps," "I was wondering if maybe") in the terse
  register — direct questions, direct asks.
- Doesn't apologize much, but when he does it's brief and specific ("Sorry for typo!!
  Please DO NOT delay.").

## Subject-line habit
ALL CAPS or a leading word flags real urgency — "IMPORTANT- TARIFFS AUGUST 19TH AT
MIDNIGHT," "HIGH IMPORTANCE: Depasquale Stock Orders." Reserve this for things that
actually are urgent; don't dilute it.
